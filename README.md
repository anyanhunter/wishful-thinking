# Wishful Thinking

A psychological horror game about working at the Wish Desk, where callers ask for help with wishes they regret.

Players review each caller's case, learn what they wished for, interpret the rules of the Wish Desk, and determine what options are available to them. What begins with strange and sometimes funny requests gradually reveals a darker story about the consequences of getting exactly what you wished for.

## Gameplay

Each case asks the player to:

- Review information about the caller and their wish
- Speak with the caller and learn more about their situation
- Interpret the rules governing wishes
- Determine what options are available
- Make a decision about how to handle the case
- Discover the outcome of that decision

The cases follow a scripted narrative. Individual cases have their own choices and outcomes, while the overall story develops as the player progresses through the game.

## The Rules

The Wish Desk operates under strict rules:

- Wishes cannot be altered after they are granted
- Each person receives only one wish
- A wish may be cancelled
- If a wish removes another person's autonomy, the available options become much more serious
- Following the rules does not always mean finding a good solution

## Features

- Case-based gameplay
- Player dialogue and decisions
- Multiple outcomes within individual cases
- Rule-based problem solving
- Scripted narrative progression
- Original characters and story
- Psychological horror with dark humor

## Why I Built It

I wanted to build something that combined software development with my interest in storytelling and game design. Wishful Thinking gave me the opportunity to take an original concept and turn it into a functional game using code.

Building it challenged me to organize narrative content, create reusable systems for presenting cases and dialogue, implement player choices and case outcomes, and structure story data in a way the game could use. It also gave me experience thinking about software from the player's perspective rather than only from a technical one.
